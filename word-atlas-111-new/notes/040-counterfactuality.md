# 040 · counterfactuality

One altered condition; a world of disciplined consequences.

## Definition

The character of considering what would be, or would have been, the case under a supposition contrary to fact, using an alternative condition to examine consequences, dependence, or possibility.

## Etymology

Built from counter-, against or contrary to, and factual, relating to facts; the abstract ending names the condition or character of such opposition.

## Meaning In Motion

If the bridge had remained open, the ambulance would have arrived earlier proposes a different antecedent and evaluates an imagined consequence. Such reasoning supports causal inquiry, planning, regret, and historical analysis, but its credibility depends on which surrounding facts remain fixed.

## The Crucial Distinction

A changed bridge need not imply changed weather or a different patient. Philosophers disagree about how best to select relevant alternatives, including approaches involving possible worlds or causal models. Counterfactuality therefore differs from unrestricted fantasy: disciplined alternatives preserve enough structure to make the comparison informative.

## Holding The Background Still

Counterfactual reasoning needs selective stability. To ask whether a bridge closure delayed an ambulance, keep relevant circumstances comparable before tracing consequences of reopening it. Changing every background condition at once would make the imagined improvement impossible to attribute to the particular intervention under consideration.

## The Danger Of Hindsight

Knowing the actual outcome can make an alternative seem more predictable than it was. Historical counterfactuals therefore require evidence about available choices and constraints. The paired routes invite comparison, but their symmetry is an analytical convenience: real histories may amplify a small difference through many interactions.

## Original literary sentence

Had the latch held, the letter would have slept beneath the door until morning, and the city we remember would have missed its appointment with a wholly different sorrow.

## Extended reading

The sentence if the match had been struck, it would have lit is neither a report of an actual flame nor an unconstrained invention. It compresses assumptions about dryness, oxygen, friction, and the match's condition. Discovering that the match was soaked changes the judgment even though its unstruck status stays the same. Background information does real explanatory work.

Different theories formalize that work differently. A possible worlds approach compares suitably similar alternatives; a causal model represents relations and asks what follows from changing a variable. Neither brief description settles the difficult question of which similarities or causal structures are justified. The relevant standard depends partly on the inquiry: physical explanation, legal responsibility, personal regret, and strategic planning ask overlapping but distinct questions.

For practice, state an alternative outcome, identify the intervention, and list three facts you are holding constant. Then vary one of those facts. If the conclusion reverses, you have located a sensitive assumption rather than necessarily disproved the whole exercise. Disciplined imagination earns its usefulness by exposing such dependencies instead of hiding them behind the apparent fluency of a story.
