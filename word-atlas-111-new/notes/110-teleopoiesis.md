# 110 · teleopoiesis

An address can make toward someone still beyond reach

## Definition

A specialized term associated with Derrida and developed by readers including Spivak, concerning making or imagining across distance, including an address toward absent others or a future community not yet constituted.

## Etymology

A modern philosophical formation drawing on Greek tēle, far off, and poiēsis, making; it should not be confused with telos, goal or end.

## Making across distance

Teleopoiesis is associated with Derrida and developed by readers including Spivak. It concerns forms of making or imagining across distance, including an address toward absent others or a future community. Its meaning depends on the particular theoretical discussion; it is not a universally standardized label for all remote communication.

## The addressee can be unfinished

A text may address readers who do not yet constitute a recognizable community. The address can help imagine a relation that existing social arrangements have not secured. This productive orientation toward a possible audience exceeds the simple act of transmitting information to a fully known recipient at another location.

## Imagination need not conquer

Imaginative engagement can attempt to encounter another world without claiming to eliminate its difference or fully possess its perspective. The distance matters to the relation being attempted. Declaring perfect empathy too quickly can erase precisely the otherness that gives the philosophical and ethical problem its significance.

## A formation needs precision

The term draws on Greek tēle, far off, and poiēsis, making. It should not be confused with telos, goal or end, despite the visual resemblance in English. Nor does it refer to supernatural action at a distance; the issue concerns address, imagination, and relations made through particular practices.

## Further exploration

Writing can address a reader the writer will never meet, and that address may help imagine a relation or community that does not yet exist. Teleopoiesis names such productive distance in particular theoretical contexts; its precise emphasis differs between Derrida's treatments and Spivak's uses in thinking about imaginative engagement with others. It is not a settled everyday synonym for empathy, nor a supernatural claim about acting remotely. A reading that imagines another's world can illustrate its concerns, provided the distance and the other's difference are not simply declared overcome.

Imagine writing a letter to readers a century from now without assuming they will inherit your categories unchanged. What would make the address hospitable to their difference, and what would merely demand that they confirm your own expectations? Such questions provide one approach to the concept without exhausting its theoretical uses. Spivak's engagements also make literary imagination and the difficulty of approaching others consequential; a brief glossary should not collapse these contexts into a slogan about connection. The unfinished bridge therefore matters as much as the approaching papers. It represents an attempt that can help constitute a relation while leaving the distant shore free to answer otherwise, or not to answer at all.

## Original literary sentence

She wrote toward the reader who had not yet become possible, laying no conquering bridge across the distance, only a shore from which another shore might someday answer without becoming hers.
