# 057 · enthymeme

Persuasion asks the listener to supply a bridge.

## Definition

A rhetorical argument whose force depends on shared assumptions, often presented with an unstated premise or conclusion; in Aristotle, a rhetorical form of deduction involving premises appropriate to practical persuasion.

## Etymology

From Greek enthymēma, a consideration or thought, related to enthymeisthai, to consider; its rhetorical sense developed into a technical account of argument.

## Meaning In Motion

She has kept every earlier promise, so trust this one invites the audience to supply a bridge: reliable past conduct provides reason to expect future reliability. The missing premise account is useful, but it does not exhaust Aristotle's treatment, which concerns rhetorical proof and its characteristic premises.

## The Crucial Distinction

An enthymeme can persuade because listeners participate in completing the reasoning. That participation also makes hidden assumptions harder to notice. Reconstructing the argument reveals whether the supplied premise is plausible, contestable, or insufficient, without confusing persuasive compression with logical certainty.

## The Audience Participates

Compression can persuade because listeners complete a plausible connection themselves. That participation gives an enthymeme rhetorical force while making its assumptions less conspicuous. Reconstructing the missing bridge does not automatically refute the argument; it identifies what the audience must accept for the conclusion to seem warranted.

## Reasons Short Of Certainty

Practical persuasion often concerns probabilities, signs, and shared judgments. An argument can be reasonable without furnishing deductive certainty. Distinguish a hidden premise from a false one, and distinguish both from the broader Aristotelian account of rhetorical proof; the familiar omission formula is useful but incomplete.

## Original literary sentence

He left the middle plank unspoken, trusting their accustomed feet to lay it down, and the whole assembly crossed from yesterday's promise to tomorrow's confidence without inspecting the timber.

## Extended reading

The sentence the candidate understands ordinary workers because she once worked in a warehouse asks its audience to contribute several connections. Experience may support understanding; the past job may be relevant to the present role; other experiences may not have erased that understanding. Making these assumptions explicit changes the quality of the discussion.

Some assumptions may be plausible but defeasible. A person can have relevant experience without representing everyone's interests, and biographical similarity does not guarantee sound policy. The enthymeme's efficiency comes partly from letting a shared inference remain unobtrusive. Its weakness may appear when different audiences supply different bridges while believing they have heard the same argument.

For practice, collect a short recommendation, advertisement, or political claim. Write the implied premise in ordinary language, then state a circumstance in which it would fail. This does not require treating every compressed argument as manipulation. Conversation would become cumbersome if every inference were fully expanded. The aim is selective attention: to notice which omitted commitments carry the weight when the consequences of accepting a claim become significant.
